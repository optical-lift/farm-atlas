# Atlas Constitution

Status: **Foundational draft v0.1**  
Date: **2026-09-04**

This document records foundational invariants. It is intentionally shorter and more stable than architecture documentation.

## C-01 — Reality outranks Atlas

Reality exists independently of Atlas, its models, its interfaces, and its conclusions. Atlas must correct itself when evidence shows that its representation is wrong.

## C-02 — Evidence and interpretation remain distinct

Observed or received evidence must remain attributable and inspectable. Derived meaning, claims, classifications, recommendations, and state changes may not erase or impersonate the evidence from which they came.

## C-03 — A person is not an account

Authentication records, usernames, email addresses, phone numbers, provider identities, and login credentials are means of access or routing. None of them is the canonical identity of a human being.

## C-04 — A device is not a person

A device may be trusted, possessed, lost, shared, revoked, replaced, or reassigned. Device possession alone does not define a person or their authority.

## C-05 — Institutions exist independently of individual users

A household, business, team, or other institution may outlast any particular account, employee, administrator, device, or software provider. Atlas must model the institution independently of those temporary attachments.

## C-06 — Authority is explicit, contextual, and revocable

The ability to act as oneself is distinct from authority to act for a household, business, team, or other institution. Authority must be attributable to a person or governed actor, scoped to a context, and capable of revocation.

## C-07 — Identity is deeper than an address

Phone numbers, email addresses, messaging handles, domains, and provider account IDs are routing aliases or external identities. They may change without changing the underlying person, institution, relationship, or conversation.

## C-08 — Communication is part of reality, not a separate data-entry layer

A communication event may itself be evidence of commitments, requests, sales, payments, decisions, schedule changes, inventory changes, problems, relationships, or other real events. Atlas must preserve the communication while allowing its consequences to enter the appropriate domains without requiring humans to re-transcribe reality.

## C-09 — External networks are replaceable boundaries

No external messaging, email, telecom, cloud, identity, AI, or software provider defines Atlas's internal identity, conversation, custody, authority, or world model. External systems are replaceable transports and boundary services.

## C-10 — Atlas-native communication must not depend on an external messaging provider

When both parties or institutions participate natively in Atlas, their communication must be representable, addressable, preservable, and deliverable within Atlas without requiring SMS, iMessage, RCS, WhatsApp, email, or another external messaging network as the canonical substrate.

## C-11 — The current interface is not Atlas

No current website, mobile interface, route structure, screen hierarchy, or application shell is assumed to be Atlas's permanent form. Atlas must remain capable of appearing through future interfaces and dedicated hardware without requiring its world model to be rebuilt around them.

## C-12 — Cloud custody is not the only possible custody

Atlas may use cloud infrastructure, but its foundational model must not assume that cloud services are the only possible place for identity, communication, state, evidence, computation, or durable custody to exist.

## C-13 — Human beings should not be forced to manage Atlas's machinery

Atlas should absorb artificial coordination complexity where it can do so truthfully and safely. People should steward their work, households, relationships, and institutions rather than continually re-entering, sequencing, routing, or reconciling information merely because software systems are fragmented.

## C-14 — Silence and intervention must be trustworthy

Atlas should speak, interrupt, escalate, or ask for judgment when reality warrants it. When it is silent, the person should be able to trust that Atlas is not merely failing to notice something it claimed responsibility for.

## C-15 — Present architecture must not foreclose user-held hardware

Atlas must remain capable of eventually existing on hardware possessed by the user or institution, including devices that participate in identity, communication, local custody, synchronization, and computation without being treated merely as thin clients of a cloud account.

## Amendment rule

These invariants may be amended only explicitly. An implementation convenience, provider limitation, migration shortcut, or current UI constraint is not sufficient reason to redefine them silently.

Reality remains the final authority.
