# Atlas Continuity Horizon

Status: **Governing horizon v0.1**  
Date: **2026-09-04**

This is not a roadmap and does not commit Atlas to shipping every item below.

Its purpose is narrower: **present architecture must not casually make these futures prohibitively difficult or conceptually impossible.**

## Communication and identity

Atlas must remain capable of evolving toward:

- Atlas-native person-to-person communication;
- Atlas-native institution-to-person and institution-to-institution communication;
- institutional communication identities independent of any employee's personal phone or email;
- people speaking in explicit capacities on behalf of households, businesses, teams, or other institutions;
- communication that carries structured institutional reality alongside human-readable language;
- replaceable gateways to SMS, RCS, iMessage, email, voice, WhatsApp, web chat, and future networks;
- stable conversations and identities that survive transport or provider replacement.

## Devices and hardware

Atlas must remain capable of evolving toward:

- multiple trusted devices belonging to one person;
- shared household and institutional devices;
- dedicated Atlas handheld hardware;
- child and teen Atlas devices governed by family authority rather than retrofitted consumer-app restrictions;
- tablets, laptops, embedded displays, and future device classes acting as projections of the same Atlas;
- hardware that can participate in identity and authority without equating device possession with personhood.

## Local custody and continuity

Atlas must remain capable of evolving toward:

- household or institutional Atlas Nodes;
- locally held copies of permitted Atlas reality;
- operation during intermittent or absent cloud connectivity;
- local queues for communication and work that reconcile when connectivity returns;
- local computation and intelligence where appropriate;
- cryptographically controlled user-held custody;
- cloud services that coordinate rather than uniquely define the user's world.

## Network independence

Atlas must remain capable of evolving toward:

- Atlas-native addressing independent of phone numbers and email addresses;
- direct Atlas-to-Atlas communication;
- ordinary internet connectivity as commodity transport;
- multiple carrier or network paths beneath one Atlas connectivity experience;
- external telecom, messaging, identity, and cloud providers being replaced without loss of canonical institutional history.

## Institutional continuity

Atlas must remain capable of:

- preserving a household or business when members, employees, administrators, devices, addresses, or providers change;
- transferring, revoking, and delegating authority without rewriting institutional identity;
- preserving provenance when operational reality originates in conversation;
- distinguishing personal identity from institutional capacity;
- surviving employee departure, device loss, account compromise, provider failure, and infrastructure migration without collapsing the institution into the failed component.

## Human experience

Atlas must remain capable of becoming a primary digital environment rather than merely another application among many.

This means current architecture must not assume that users will forever need separate apps for messaging, task entry, CRM updates, scheduling, finance organization, inventory updates, project status, or other busywork when those functions can become coherent expressions of one shared world model.

The target is not to recreate every incumbent application inside Atlas.

The target is to make many of those application boundaries unnecessary.

## Review question

When a foundational design choice is proposed, ask:

> If Atlas eventually lives on dedicated user-held hardware and operates a native communications network, does this choice still make sense—or are we accidentally encoding a temporary web/cloud/provider assumption as permanent reality?

A choice may still be correct even if it is temporary. The requirement is that its temporary nature be explicit.
