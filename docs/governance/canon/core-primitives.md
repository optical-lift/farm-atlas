# Atlas Canon — Core Primitives

Status: **Seed canon v0.1**  
Date: **2026-09-04**

This file defines only concepts that are already clear enough to govern present work. It is not a complete ontology.

## Person

A human being represented in Atlas independently of any login, provider account, phone number, email address, or device.

## Household

A governed human institution organized around shared life, relationship, care, resources, and authority. A household is not reducible to one owner's account.

## Institution

A durable social or operating body that may hold identity, authority, obligations, relationships, communication endpoints, assets, work, and history independently of any particular member or administrator.

Examples may include businesses, teams, households, ministries, or other governed organizations.

## Capacity

The contextual role or standing in which a person acts.

A person may act as themselves, as a parent, as an owner, as an employee, as a salesperson, or in another capacity. Capacity does not itself grant unlimited authority.

## Authority

A governed permission to observe, decide, communicate, commit, modify, delegate, or otherwise act within a defined scope.

Authority must be attributable, contextual, and revocable.

## Device

A physical or virtual computing endpoint authorized to participate in Atlas.

A device may belong to, be entrusted to, or be shared among people or institutions. It is not itself a person and possession alone does not establish authority.

## Endpoint

A routable communication identity or boundary through which a person or institution may send or receive communication.

An endpoint may be Atlas-native or external. Phone numbers, email addresses, messaging handles, and future network addresses are endpoint representations, not canonical person or institution identity.

## Conversation

A coherent communication relationship among participants and/or institutions over time.

A conversation is not defined solely by a provider thread ID, phone number pair, inbox folder, or transport. Provider threads may be evidence about a conversation without becoming its canonical identity.

## Communication Event

An attributable act of communication occurring between one or more participants or capacities.

A communication event may contain human language, media, structured payloads, reply ancestry, delivery information, and references to other Atlas reality. The event remains distinguishable from any interpretation or domain consequence derived from it.

## Evidence

An attributable observation, record, communication, artifact, source event, or other retained basis for knowing something about reality.

Evidence must preserve provenance and must not be silently replaced by conclusions drawn from it.

## Derived Proposition

A statement Atlas infers, recognizes, extracts, or proposes from evidence.

A derived proposition may be uncertain, provisional, accepted, rejected, superseded, or promoted according to governing domain rules. It is never identical to its source evidence.

## Domain State

Canonical operational truth governed by the rules and authority of a specific Atlas domain.

Examples may include an accepted order, inventory position, payment state, scheduled obligation, relationship state, or project state.

Communication ingestion does not automatically possess authority to mutate domain state. Promotion from evidence or proposition into canonical state must occur through the relevant domain boundary.

## Relationship

A durable, inspectable connection among people, households, institutions, capacities, objects, or events that has meaning in the world model.

Relationships should be represented according to reality rather than inferred solely from shared accounts, addresses, providers, or UI grouping.

## Governing rule for canon growth

New canonical primitives should be added only when Atlas repeatedly needs to reason about something as a distinct real thing.

Do not create a canonical concept merely because a provider, framework, database, screen, or API happens to expose a similarly named object.
